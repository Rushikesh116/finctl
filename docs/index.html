What this page is

Why matching a bank credit to the sales behind it is harder than looking up a reference number.

This shop made 225 sales in a month. Its bank statement for the same month has 33 credits on it.

That gap is the whole problem. The payment company does not pay out one sale at a time. It gathers up a batch, subtracts its own fee and the tax on that fee, subtracts anything that was refunded, and sends a single amount. So one line on the bank statement is dozens of sales added together and then reduced by charges that are written down nowhere on that line.

Checking one of those credits is therefore not a lookup. There is no reference number that leads from the bank line back to the sales inside it. The question is which set of transactions adds up to exactly this amount — and you cannot answer it by matching one row to one row.

Those last 4 rows carry no reference, no batch number, nothing at all tying them to this payout. They were found by searching for a combination that closes the gap to the paisa, and they were only accepted after the total was recomputed against the bank figure and matched exactly. A lookup finds 4 of the 8 rows behind this credit. The rest have to be worked out.

FinCtl does this for every credit on the statement, and where the records genuinely do not settle the question, it says so rather than guessing. Click any row in the list below to see the records and the reasoning behind that decision.
